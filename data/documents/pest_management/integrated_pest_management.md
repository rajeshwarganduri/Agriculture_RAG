# Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Integrated Pest Management combines monitoring, prevention, cultural practices, biological controls, and careful pesticide use when necessary. The aim is to manage pests while reducing avoidable risks to people, beneficial organisms, and the environment.

## IPM steps
1. Inspect fields regularly and identify the pest correctly.
2. Record the affected crop stage, symptoms, and extent of damage.
3. Use prevention such as crop rotation, field sanitation, resistant varieties, and suitable planting dates where recommended.
4. Encourage natural enemies and consider approved biological controls where appropriate.
5. Use an economic threshold or local advisory before deciding on treatment.
6. If a pesticide is necessary, use only a product registered for the crop and target pest, follow the label, wear required protective equipment, and observe re-entry and pre-harvest intervals.

Never mix products or recommend a dose without checking the approved product label and local agricultural guidance. If identification is uncertain, consult an extension officer or plant clinic.
